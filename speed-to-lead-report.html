How the cutoff was chosen

  • Raw hours are heavily right-skewed (skewness 7.0), so the mean mostly reflects the tail.
  • A log-scale outlier test flagged nothing. After a log transform the data is nearly symmetric (skewness 0.13), so the tail is the natural shape of the data and not bad records.
  • The cutoff is a Tukey upper fence on raw hours: Q3 + 1.5 × IQR = 45 + 1.5 × 45 = 113 hours (4.7 days), set once across all sources.
  • The exclusion is a presentation choice. Those 120 deals are mostly booked or quoted customers who simply took longer.
  • Offline and many short Meta values are contacts created already booked, so a median near zero partly reflects how the record was created. Excluding anything under 5 minutes, the trimmed Meta median is 8.3 hours.

Result across other cutoffs

All sources, 674 deals. The conclusion does not depend on the exact cutoff.
Findings

Key directional insights

Booking

Meta books at 60%, other sources at 81 to 88%. The gap is widest in Jan to May and Sep. Meta leads with a non-marketing flag (116) booked at 13% and skew to Not Interested, OOT and Cold.

Speed

Typical Meta deal appears in 3.4 hours. Across all sources the typical lead is booked in under an hour, so Meta is the slow channel.

Coverage gap

Weekends and evenings are the weak spot. Weekend Meta leads take 17 hours at the median and 6 to 9 PM leads take 14 hours, against 1.4 and 0.6 hours in the daytime.

Long tail

One in four Meta leads takes more than 4.7 days to book. 53 Meta leads are still at New Lead after about 9 touches each, and 30 more are Cold Lead.

Calls

When a first call is logged, it works. The deal usually follows within minutes. Logging is too thin (66 contacts) to measure speed to contact.

Cost

About $99 per booked Meta assessment. Raising the Meta booking rate to the other sources' level would cut that cost by about a quarter.

Suggested next steps

  • Cover weekends and evenings. Use an auto-reply with a booking link, or extend CSR hours, for Meta leads created after 6 PM and on Saturday and Sunday.
  • Audit the marketing contact flag. Meta leads should all be marketing contacts. Find what sets non-marketing in HubSpot, and fix it so source reporting stays clean.
  • Review Meta targeting and form questions with the agency, using out-of-territory and Not Interested rates as the quality signal.
  • Work the stalled leads. Triage the 53 Meta New Lead and 30 Cold Lead contacts for reactivation or clean-up.
  • Log the first call every time. Without a timestamp, speed to first contact cannot be measured. Revisit in 60 to 90 days.

Data notes

  • Lead status is a current snapshot. Cancelled assessments, declined quotes and OOT assessments do not appear as statuses, so the booking rate may be slightly low.
  • Time to deal depends on how and when deals were created in HubSpot. It matches the Customer timing for 603 of 707 leads that have both.
  • CPL assumes all $27K went to Meta. If some went to search, Meta CPL falls and the other numbers move slightly.
  • The marketing contact flag is not used to define Meta. All 455 Paid Social leads count as Meta, including 116 flagged non-marketing, which is likely a tagging or cleanup effect. 89 of those were created Jan to May.
  • 5 emails and 51 phone numbers are duplicated across the 2026 contacts and were not de-duplicated.
