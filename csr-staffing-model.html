How the model works

  • Arrivals. Leads per hour follow your 2026 time-of-day pattern. Leads outside coverage hours are called at opening. A burst factor covers spikes.
  • First call. Connected calls take talk plus wrap-up time. Unanswered attempts take about 2 minutes. Callbacks share the same agents.
  • Queueing. Erlang C, the standard call-center method, finds the fewest agents each hour so the chosen share of first calls starts within the target time.
  • Follow-ups. Unreached leads get follow-up attempts over the next days, which fill idle time.
  • People. Agent-hours per week divided by paid hours after shrinkage, plus supervisors.
  • Connect rates. My assumption: 40%, 35% and 28% on the first attempt by target, and 15% on each follow-up. That reaches 56 to 63% of leads, near the 52% of Meta leads reached today.
  • Not modeled. Time zones (coverage hours are Eastern), attrition and training, quality assurance, dialer tooling, technician capacity and overnight leads, which an automatic text can handle.
