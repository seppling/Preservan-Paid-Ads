How staffing scales with locations and lead volume

Inputs

How the model works

  • Lead volume. Item 19 shows 2025 leads per day per outlet from 1.9 (Athens) to 3.7 (Orlando), averaging 2.6 across the seven outlets. The model starts at 2.6, and about 20% of leads become service visits.
  • Arrivals. Leads per hour follow the time-of-day pattern of your 2026 in-territory leads. Leads that arrive outside coverage hours are called when the team opens, which creates a morning surge. A burst factor covers ad-driven spikes.
  • First call. Each new lead needs an immediate call. Calls that connect take talk plus wrap-up time. Calls that do not connect take about 2 minutes for the dial, voicemail, text and log.
  • Queueing. I used the Erlang C formula, the standard call-center method, to find the fewest agents per hour so that the chosen share of first calls (default 90%) starts within the target time. Callbacks from leads who missed a call share the same agents.
  • Follow-ups. Leads not reached get follow-up attempts over the next days. These are not time-critical, so they fill idle time. If total workload divided by the occupancy cap is higher than the seats needed for speed, workload sets the staffing.
  • From seats to people. Agent-hours per week divided by paid hours per FTE after shrinkage (breaks, PTO, training, meetings). Supervisors are added at the span of control you choose.
  • Connect rates. I assumed faster calling connects more often (first attempt 40%, 35%, 28%) and each later attempt connects 15% of those remaining. That gives 56 to 63% of leads reached overall, close to the 52% of Meta leads reached today after a median of 8 touches.

Variables to add or check

  • Time zones. If locations span Eastern to Pacific, 7 AM Eastern to 9 PM Pacific is 17 hours. Set coverage hours in Eastern time to match.
  • Seat floors. One always-on seat for 15 hours, 7 days a week is about 3.75 FTE. At this volume the floor drives staffing for the slower targets.
  • Burst risk. Ad pushes, promotions and weather events create spikes. Raise the burst factor if leads cluster.
  • Overnight leads. About 15% of leads arrive between 10 PM and 7 AM. An automatic text with a booking link is a cheaper answer than overnight calling.
  • Dialer and tooling. A power dialer, click-to-call from HubSpot and SMS automation cut unanswered-call time and cut the per-lead workload.
  • Attrition and ramp. Remote CSR teams often turn over 30 to 50% a year. Plan for hiring 2 to 3 months ahead and for partial productivity while training.
  • Quality assurance. Call review and coaching need time beyond supervisors. 1 supervisor per 10 to 12 agents is a common ratio.
  • Lead quality. Misrouted, duplicate and spam leads still consume calls. The uplift input covers this. Fixing territory routing would lower it.
  • Local trust. Local numbers, a local-sounding first text and a named local contact change connect and booking rates, not staffing.
